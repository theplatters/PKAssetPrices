\begin{abstract}
This teaching note examines the treatment of money and speculation in the \treatise{} and the \GT{}. We distinguish the two works along two independent dimensions, namely whether money is endogenous or exogenous and whether speculative markets feed back into real economic activity. Sorting both works into the resulting 2\,$\times$\,2 typology shows that each book occupies a different cell, and that a coherent third position, endogenous money combined with real feedback from speculation, is left open.

Building on \citet{fontanaSimpleTeachableMacroeconomic2009}, we fill this gap with a compact Post-Keynesian framework in which loans create deposits, extended step by step with a speculative asset market. Switching on a single equation at a time then makes visible three distinct channels through which speculation shapes output and employment, namely a credit-rationing channel, a central bank interest-rate channel, and a financialization-of-firms channel. A policy variant on dual interest rates shows how a premium on speculative credit dampens asset prices without touching the productive lending rate, and recent experience, from the 2020--21 ``everything rally'' to the 2022--23 crypto winter, illustrates the mechanisms and their limits.
\end{abstract}
